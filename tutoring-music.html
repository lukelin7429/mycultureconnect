Music & Instruments

Music is personal, so students usually have plenty to say. Talk about favorite songs and singers, learn the names of instruments, then finish a short song together. Share your own music too — the goal is a real two-way conversation, not a test.

⏱ About 60 minutes 🎯 All levels (questions are leveled) · works well at B1 📋 No prep — tap 🔊 to hear any phrase
Before you start Warm-up Key words Instruments Read & talk Let's talk Song activity Wrap-up Tutor notes

Before you start tutor prep

  • Open this page and turn on your camera and microphone before the student joins. Ask the student to keep their camera on, too.
  • Pick one song you love and have it ready to play for about 20 seconds (YouTube or Spotify). When you share your screen, turn on "share sound."
  • Ask the student before class (or at the start) to bring one song they like.
  • Keep a pen and paper (or a notes window) handy to write down new words.
  • Plan to talk about half the time. Ask a question, listen, then add your own answer.
Mindset: You don't need to be musical to teach this lesson. Curiosity is enough — ask about the student's music and let them be the expert.

1 · Warm-up 5 min

Play 20 seconds of your song. Then ask:

  • Did you like it? Was it fast or slow? Happy or sad?
  • What kind of music do you think it is?
  • When do you usually listen to music?
  • Can you play me 20 seconds of a song you like?
Tip: Playing a real song breaks the ice faster than any question. React honestly to the student's song — "Oh, I like the beat!" — before you ask anything else.

2 · Key words & phrases 7 min

Read each word aloud, tap 🔊 so the student hears it, and have them repeat it. Then ask them to make one sentence about their own music.

  • genrePop is my favorite genre of music.
  • singerShe is my favorite singer.
  • bandMy brother plays the drums in a band.
  • lyricsI like the music, but I don't understand the lyrics.
  • melodyThe melody is easy to remember.
  • rhythmThis song has a fast rhythm, so I want to dance.
  • concertI went to a concert in Taipei last year.
  • instrumentDo you play an instrument?
  • play the ___I played the piano when I was a child.
  • practiceI practice the guitar for twenty minutes every day.
  • "This song makes me feel ___"This song makes me feel relaxed.
  • "I'm into ___"I am into K-pop right now.

Common genres to mention: pop, rock, hip-hop, classical, jazz, K-pop, Mandopop.

Tip: "Lyrics," "melody," and "rhythm" are easy to mix up. Play your song again and point: "The words are the lyrics. The tune you can hum is the melody. The beat you can clap is the rhythm."

3 · Instruments 8 min

Go through the instruments together. For each one, ask: "Have you heard it? Have you played it? Do you like how it sounds?"

  • guitarStrings — you play it with your fingers.
  • violinStrings — you play it with a bow.
  • pianoKeyboard — it has black and white keys.
  • drumsPercussion — you hit them to keep the beat.
  • fluteWind — you blow across it.
  • trumpetBrass — it is loud and bright.
  • erhu (二胡)A Chinese instrument with two strings. 二胡
  • guzheng (古箏)A long Chinese zither with many strings. 古箏

Mini game: Look up a short video of one instrument and play the sound only (hide the picture). The student guesses which instrument it is, then describes the sound: soft, loud, high, low, bright, sad.

Tip: The erhu and guzheng are a great chance to flip roles — let the student teach you about Taiwanese and Chinese instruments. Many students learned one at school.

4 · Read & talk 7 min

Read this together. You take A, the student takes B, then swap roles. Tap 🔊 on any line the student finds tricky.

A:

What kind of music are you into these days?

B:

Mostly pop. I listen to it on the bus every morning.

A:

Nice! Who is your favorite singer?

B:

I really like a Taiwanese singer. Her songs have beautiful melodies.

A:

Do you understand all the lyrics in English songs?

B:

Not all of them. Sometimes I look them up online.

A:

Good idea. Do you play an instrument?

B:

I played the piano when I was little, but I stopped. Do you?

A:

I play the guitar a little. I practice on weekends.

B:

Cool! Maybe you can play a song for me next time.

Tip: After reading, ask the same questions for real: "What kind of music are you into these days?" Let the student answer about themselves, not the script.

5 · Let's talk 10 min

Pick questions that fit your student's level. Answer some yourself, too.

BEGINNER
  • Do you like music? What kind?
  • Who is your favorite singer?
  • Do you sing? Where — in the shower, at KTV?
  • Do you play an instrument?
INTERMEDIATE
  • What song do you listen to when you are happy? When you are tired?
  • Have you ever been to a concert? What was it like?
  • Which instrument would you like to learn, and why?
  • What music is popular with young people in Taiwan right now?
  • Do you listen to music while you study? Does it help?
ADVANCED
  • Is a good melody or good lyrics more important in a song?
  • Should every child learn an instrument at school? Why or why not?
  • How has the way people listen to music changed in the last 20 years?
  • Can music help people learn a language? How?
Tip: KTV is a big part of life in Taiwan. Asking "What do you sing at KTV?" usually gets a long, happy answer.

6 · Song activity 18 min

Part A · Fill in the blanks 8 min

Jingle Bells (James Lord Pierpont, 1857 — public domain). Share your screen. The student reads the lyrics and chooses a word from the box for each blank. Then play the song (any version on YouTube) and check the answers together.

waysnowfunsinglaughingfieldsbrighttonight

Dashing through the ___

In a one-horse open sleigh,

O'er the ___ we go,

___ all the way.

Bells on bobtail ring,

Making spirits ___.

What fun it is to ride and ___

A sleighing song ___!

 

Jingle bells, jingle bells,

Jingle all the ___.

Oh, what ___ it is to ride

In a one-horse open sleigh!

Answer key (tutor only)

snow · fields · laughing · bright · sing · tonight · way · fun

Words to explain: sleigh — a cart that slides on snow, pulled by a horse; o'er — old word for "over"; bobtail — a horse with a short tail; spirits bright — everyone feels happy.

Part B · Finish the song 10 min

A short, simple song (written for MCC). Each verse is missing its last word. The student chooses a word that rhymes and makes sense. There is more than one good answer!

When I wake up in the morning, I turn my music on,

I sing it in the shower, I sing until it's ___.

 

I listen on the bus, I listen on the train,

I listen in the sunshine, I listen in the ___.

 

My sister plays the drums, my brother likes to sing,

My dad plays the guitar, and I can play ___!

Possible answers (tutor only)

gone · rain · anything (or "the ___" — any instrument, if the student doesn't mind breaking the rhyme)

Now write your own verse. The student fills in the frame below. Then read it aloud together — or sing it to any melody you both know!

When I feel ___, I listen to ___.

It makes me feel ___, and I want to ___.

Tip: Rhyming is hard in a second language, so help by saying a few words that rhyme ("rain, train, plane…") and letting the student pick. Praise creative answers even if they don't rhyme perfectly. If the student is shy about singing, reading it like a poem is fine.

7 · Wrap-up 5 min

  • Ask the student to name two instruments and one new word they learned today.
  • Ask them to recommend one song for you to listen to this week, and you recommend one back.
  • Give one piece of warm, specific praise ("Your verse was really creative!").
  • Tiny homework: find the lyrics to one English song they like and bring three new words next time.
Tip: Start the next class by asking about the song you recommended to each other. It's an easy, natural warm-up.

🧭 Tutor notes

BEGINNER
  • Focus on the instrument names and "I like ___ music." Use pictures or sounds instead of explanations.
  • In Part A, give the first letter of each missing word as a hint.
  • Skip "write your own verse" or do it together, one line at a time.
INTERMEDIATE (B1)
  • Push for reasons: "Why do you like that singer?" "How does this song make you feel?"
  • Do both parts of the song activity. Ask the student to explain why a word fits.
  • Let small errors slide; recast them naturally instead of stopping to correct.
ADVANCED
  • Bring a short pop song and look at the lyrics together: what is the story, and what do the metaphors mean?
  • Ask the student to write a full four-line verse with a rhyme pattern.
  • Note two or three advanced phrases at the end for them to keep.
About song choice: For fill-in-the-blank lyrics on screen, stick to public-domain songs like the one above (or ones you wrote yourself). For modern songs, play the official video and talk about it rather than copying the lyrics into your own worksheet.
